05

Monitoring

Monitor the crop from establishment through harvest. A repeatable scouting route helps you distinguish an isolated problem from a spreading pattern.

  1. Inspect representative plants in different parts of the field, including edges and wetter or weaker patches. Look at leaves, stems, growing points, and the root zone where practical.
  2. Record growth stage, moisture, weeds, pest signs, beneficial organisms, and symptoms. Photograph changes from the same position and note recent weather or input applications.
  3. Identify the cause before deciding on treatment. Use the crop’s pest reference or leaf-photo tool for investigation and ask a local expert when symptoms remain uncertain.

06

{% translate 'Weeding' %}

Keep weed management suited to the crop’s root system, growing stage, and field layout. Preventing weeds from setting seed reduces pressure on later crops.

  1. Identify weeds and remove young growth before it becomes difficult to control. Give newly established crop plants particular attention because weeds compete for water, nutrients, and light.
  2. {% if crop.name == 'Rice' %}Use a weed-management method suited to your rice system and row layout. Coordinate weeding with water management rather than copying dry-land cultivation methods.{% elif crop.name == 'Lotus' %}Manage unwanted aquatic growth and crowding without damaging rhizomes or growing tips. Follow the listed thinning practice and maintain access to inspect plants.{% else %}Use hand removal or careful shallow cultivation where suitable. Avoid injuring crop roots or stems, and use clean mulch only where it fits the crop and field conditions.{% endif %}
  3. Recheck weed regrowth after rain or irrigation. Keep tools clean when moving between fields and remove weeds before adding nutrients to the crop root zone.

Check: Crop-root damage, remaining weeds, and new weed emergence.

08

Organic fertilization

Plan organic nutrient inputs as part of the crop’s total nutrient budget. Organic materials vary in nutrient concentration and release rate, so equal weights do not supply equal nutrition.

  1. Use the soil test and a crop-specific recommendation to identify the nutrient need. Record previous compost or manure inputs before deciding what to add.
  2. Choose mature, well-composted material from a reliable source. Check its quality and nutrient analysis where available; handle and store it to prevent contamination of crops and water.
  3. Use a locally advised rate, timing, and placement for this crop. Include nutrients contributed by compost or manure when calculating any additional fertilizer requirement.

A verified organic application rate is not provided in this crop record. Obtain a recommendation for your soil, crop stage, and material before applying.

09

Chemical fertilization

Mineral fertilizer supplies particular nutrients; it should address a measured need rather than substitute for diagnosing poor growth.

  1. Start with a current soil test and a recommendation for the crop, growth stage, and expected production. Account for organic inputs and previous applications.
  2. Check whether the recommendation is expressed per hectare, per plant, or per tree, and whether phosphorus and potassium are reported as P2O5 and K2O. Enter matching values in the calculator.
  3. Review product grades and calculated weights. Follow locally recommended split doses and placement, avoid contact that can injure seeds or plant tissue, and keep an application record.

Preventive measures

  1. Begin with healthy planting material, clean tools, and a layout that supports appropriate light, airflow, and crop access. Avoid moving contaminated soil or plant material into clean areas.
  2. Maintain suitable water and nutrition rather than stressing the crop with excess or shortage. Inspect new planting stock and act on confirmed problems before they spread.

11

Chemical plant protection

Consider chemical control only after identifying the problem and deciding that intervention is needed. A symptom, photo result, or calendar date alone is not a spray recommendation.

  1. Confirm the pest or disease and discuss the need for treatment with a local crop expert. Check whether suitable non-chemical measures can address the problem.
  2. If a pesticide is advised, verify current local approval for this crop and target. Follow the product label for formulation, dose, protective equipment, application, re-entry, and the interval before harvest.
  3. Check actual weather and nearby people, water, and pollinator activity. Record the product and application, store it in its original container, and follow label directions for disposal and follow-up monitoring.

No pesticide product or spray dose is prescribed here. Use current locally approved guidance and the product label.

Harvesting

  1. {% if crop.group == 'Cereals' or crop.group == 'Pulses' or crop.group == 'Oilseeds' %}For grain or seed production, assess maturity and grain or seed moisture using the crop’s harvest guidance. 13

Post-harvest

  1. Sort the harvest promptly, remove damaged material, and keep each lot identified. Use clean handling surfaces, packaging, and transport suited to the product.
  2. {% if crop.group == 'Cereals' or crop.group == 'Pulses' or crop.group == 'Oilseeds' %}Clean and dry grain or seed to the crop’s verified storage-moisture target before storage. Prevent re-wetting, keep bags away from ground moisture, and inspect for heating, mould, insects, or rodents.{% elif crop.group == 'Flowers' %}Grade blooms or stems to the buyer’s specification and protect them from crushing and water loss. Use the crop’s recommended conditioning, packaging, and temperature requirements for the intended flower product.{% elif crop.name == 'Onion' or crop.name == 'Garlic' or crop.name == 'Potato' or crop.name == 'Sweet potato' or crop.name == 'Ginger' or crop.name == 'Turmeric' %}Confirm whether the intended product needs curing, drying, fresh marketing, or another processing step. Follow the crop’s handling requirements; avoid applying a generic washing or cold-storage method.{% elif crop.group == 'Fruits' or crop.group == 'Vegetables' %}Limit field heat and rough handling. Confirm the crop-specific cooling, washing, packaging, and storage requirements; one temperature or washing method does not suit every fruit or vegetable.{% else %}Follow the processing route for the harvested product, such as fresh delivery, drying, or other crop-specific handling. Confirm quality and moisture specifications with the reference source and buyer.{% endif %}
  3. Store away from fertilizers, pesticides, animals, and contaminated equipment. Inspect stored lots and keep dispatch, quality, and loss records for the next season’s planning.
